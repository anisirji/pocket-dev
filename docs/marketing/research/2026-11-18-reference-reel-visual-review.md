# Reference reels: first visual review

Reviewed from the three downloaded MP4s, not from link descriptions alone. Method: extracted opening frames at two frames per second and contact sheets spanning each video. Read visible captions and diagrams. This is a sampled visual review; audio has not yet been transcribed or reviewed in full. Durations come from ffprobe. No source performance data is available, so these observations do not establish that a format will improve Pocket Dev's retention.

## 1. minutelet — Det6xAWgLk4 — 93.18 seconds

**Observed opening:** a bridge carrying more and more users, accompanied by a rising user counter. It sags, turns red, and collapses at 1,000. The captioned opening warns that the viewer's app is not ready for that many users. A checklist then introduces development problems; the example develops into race conditions using the last available ticket and two purchase requests.

**Visual format:** blue blueprint background, white hand-drawn diagrams, yellow emphasized words, moving explanatory elements, and captions close to the diagrams. Ticket confirmations, database state, purchase requests, and duplicate outcomes make an abstract programming problem visible. Later sampled frames contain a prompt to ask an AI, then a preview of another development problem.

**Reason to continue, inferred:** the opening depicts a recognizable consequence and raises the question of what could break the viewer's own app. The ticket scenario gives the explanation a specific outcome to resolve.

**What to borrow:** one concrete visual metaphor for the opening; turn the developer's problem into an observable failure or obstacle; show one causal sequence; deliver a usable takeaway.

**Pocket Dev adaptation direction:** depict the user moving away while their coding conversation remains on the computer. Then show the actual supported conversation opened and continued on a phone. Keep the demonstration visible and readable; the blueprint style must help explain the workflow.

**Candidate opening:** “Your coding session is still at your desk.” Visual: a person leaves while the active session remains attached to the desk. Follow with the phone opening that conversation. The host remains awake and reachable throughout.

## 2. Belltake — DfhNx3Lh0GP — 84.33 seconds

**Observed opening:** a night scene with a drone, gunfire, an explosion, and people on a vehicle. Early captions establish night and an absence of radar. Later sampled frames reveal a microphone and phone on a pole, illustrate terrain hiding objects, then explain a network through maps, lines, signal graphics, and dated scenes.

**Visual format:** cinematic illustrated scenes with dark colors, serif captions, selected orange words, diagram overlays, maps, and source/date labels.

**Reason to continue, inferred:** the opening starts with an event and leaves a mechanism unexplained. Subsequent scenes promise to explain how an unexpectedly simple component contributes to a larger system.

**What to borrow:** open in the middle of a real situation; reveal the useful mechanism progressively; alternate story scenes and explanatory diagrams.

**Pocket Dev adaptation direction:** a developer away from their desk needs to continue a task. Reveal the phone interface, explain that the existing computer runs the work, and prove the completed next action. Borrow the narrative structure, not the military subject matter or unsupported product claims.

## 3. Satellite Digital Co — Df1gkaiGSh1 — 108.84 seconds

**Observed opening:** a five-stage awareness title, a customer/profile icon, a line of small audience dots, and an offer label directed toward a small cluster at the end. The early captions discuss advertising aimed at people already ready to buy. Later diagrams split the audience into stages and connect different messages to different groups.

**Visual format:** black background, sparse line icons, small white dots, red emphasis, restrained diagram changes, and short caption lines. The same visual system develops over successive scenes.

**Reason to continue, inferred:** it identifies a possible mismatch between a marketer's message and the audience's current understanding, then offers a framework to diagnose it.

**What to borrow:** explain one idea through a diagram that develops with the argument; introduce each element when needed; match message and next step to what the viewer already understands.

**Pocket Dev adaptation direction:** map the path from computer-only work to phone continuity. Start with the recognizable interruption, explain the computer/phone relationship, and end with actual product proof. Avoid opening with an unexplained feature list.
